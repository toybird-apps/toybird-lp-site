Compare Quotes with ChatGPT: Put Three Proposals on the Same Basis

A per-session price, an all-inclusive quote and a per-person fee cannot be compared by choosing the smallest number on the page. First calculate the same required service, then check its mandatory conditions.

The following training procurement example is fictional. Amounts are in Japanese yen, excluding tax, solely to demonstrate a calculation. They are not market prices, supplier recommendations or measured AI results.

AI Work Brief case

This article is one concrete AI Work Brief case. The useful part is not a magic phrase: it is deciding what the AI may use, what must stay unresolved, and what counts as a finished result before drafting begins.

See the AI Work Brief framework

The decision is a 30-person course, not a headline price

The organization needs training for 30 people, completed by 20 October, within JPY 450,000. Thirty days of questions and answers after the course is mandatory.

Quote Information supplied
A JPY 180,000 per session; capacity 10. Three identical sessions serve 30 people. All three can finish before the deadline. Thirty-day questions and answers are included. No additional fees.
B JPY 420,000 for all 30 people. Delivery before the deadline and thirty-day questions and answers are confirmed. No additional fees.
C JPY 9,000 per person plus JPY 60,000 setup. Supports 30 participants. Delivery dates and the duration of questions and answers are not stated. No other fees are listed.

A is not a JPY 180,000 solution for this group. Equally, C's missing support information does not establish that C offers no support.

Keep mandatory requirements out of a weighted average

A low price cannot compensate for a mandatory condition that is not met. Separate requirements from preferences before requesting a recommendation.

Compare these three training quotes for an internal selection meeting.
Requirements: 30 participants; total no more than JPY 450,000 excluding tax; completion by 20 October; 30 days of questions and answers after training.
Use only the supplied quote information.
1. Calculate the total for 30 people and show each formula.
2. For each mandatory requirement, mark met, not met, or unconfirmed.
3. Do not treat missing information as “not offered” or zero points.
4. Separate candidates that can currently be assessed from those requiring clarification.
5. Draft the questions needed before a decision.
Do not select a supplier solely because its stated price is lowest.

B is the only option whose mandatory conditions are all confirmed. That does not prove B is the best supplier in every respect, or that C is unsuitable. It tells the decision-maker what the evidence currently permits.

Check the multiplication yourself or in a spreadsheet. In a real comparison, attach the quotation name, date and section to every material row. A neat table is not a substitute for that traceability.

Turn missing cells into a useful supplier email

We are considering training for 30 participants. Can it be completed by 20 October? Can participants ask questions for 30 days after the course? If both are possible, please confirm whether they are included in the quoted JPY 330,000 total.

This asks about the two conditions blocking the decision. “Please send more information” may simply produce another brochure. If the reply introduces an additional fee, update the calculation as well as the support cell.

Reconsider preferences without moving the goalposts

Keep participant count, completion date and thirty-day question support mandatory.
If C confirms these conditions, compare the eligible options with total price as the first preference.
Do not treat current unknowns as confirmed facts.
State which new information changed the conclusion.

If the budget itself changes, record that separately. Otherwise a later “better recommendation” might merely be answering a different question. This approach works for subscriptions, equipment or services too, provided you normalize the actual billing unit and do not invent missing prices.

Use Prompt Ready to prepare changing comparison conditions

The reusable part is not a verdict about A, B or C. It is the structure: participants, deadline, mandatory conditions, pricing unit and how to handle missing facts.
