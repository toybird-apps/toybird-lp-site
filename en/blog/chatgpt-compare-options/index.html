How to compare options with ChatGPT: criteria, comparison table and recommendation prompt

Make the decision criteria visible before asking the AI to recommend an option.

August 3, 202610 min readBy Masaki Iino

This guide shows a practical way to use ChatGPT at work. The goal is not to memorize a magic prompt, but to give the AI enough context, constraints, and an output format that match the task.

You can copy the prompt example, replace the bracketed details, and then review the result before using it in real work.

Key takeaways

  • Provide the same factual fields for every option.
  • Define the criteria and which ones matter most for your situation.
  • Ask the AI to show missing or incomparable information.
  • Separate facts from the evaluation and recommendation.
  • Review the recommendation against source documents and current prices.

A recommendation without criteria often reflects hidden assumptions

Why a short instruction often fails

Compare these three options and choose the best one.

A more reliable approach

Define who the decision is for, the criteria, priorities, constraints, and how missing information should affect the recommendation.

Information to provide

  • The options and their source information
  • The decision maker and use case
  • Comparison criteria and required fields
  • Priority, budget, risk tolerance, and non-negotiable conditions
  • Rules for missing data, evidence, and recommendation

Reusable prompt template

Replace the bracketed parts with your situation. Ask the AI not to invent missing facts and to list questions separately.

Reusable prompt template
## Task
Compare the options below and support a decision.

## Decision context
- Decision maker: [person or team]
- Use case: [what the option will be used for]
- Non-negotiable conditions: [list]
- Highest priorities: [ranked list]

## Options and source information
[Paste the information for each option]

## Comparison criteria
- [criterion 1]
- [criterion 2]
- [criterion 3]
- [criterion 4]

## Output
1. Comparison table
2. Strengths and drawbacks of each option
3. Missing or incomparable information
4. Recommendation and reasons
5. Conditions that would change the recommendation

## Constraints
- Do not invent missing specifications, prices, or reviews.
- Separate source facts from your evaluation.
- If the evidence is insufficient, say that no final recommendation can be made.

Example: compare training providers for a team of beginners

A 30-person company wants generative AI training. Continuous participation by beginners matters more than the lowest price. The options differ in support, class size, schedule, and follow-up.

Practical example
Compare the three providers on price, participant limit, support, implementation time, beginner suitability, and follow-up. Our company has 30 employees and many are new to generative AI. Give more weight to beginner support and continued use than to the lowest price. Show missing information and explain which conditions could change the recommendation.

Practical example

The table shows the available facts for all three providers. Provider B is the provisional recommendation because its onboarding and follow-up best match the stated priority. The recommendation remains provisional until class-size limits and post-training support are confirmed.

Review before using the result

Treat the result as a draft. Confirm names, dates, prices, commitments, and company policy before sending or acting on it.

  • Every option was evaluated using the same criteria.
  • Priorities and non-negotiable conditions are visible.
  • Missing data is shown instead of being scored as a fact.
  • The recommendation follows the stated criteria rather than generic popularity.
  • Prices, specifications, and availability are checked against current sources.
Do not paste personal data, confidential customer information, contract details, or unreleased company information unless your organization allows it. Replace names and figures with placeholders when needed.

Frequently asked questions

Can ChatGPT choose the best product for me?

It can organize evidence and show a recommendation based on your stated criteria. A person should verify current facts and make the final decision.

How should I handle missing information?

Ask for a separate “missing information” section and conditions under which the current recommendation would change.

Should I use a score for each option?

A score can make priorities visible, but define the weighting and avoid false precision when the underlying information is uncertain.
