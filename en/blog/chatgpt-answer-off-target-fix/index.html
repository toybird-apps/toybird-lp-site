Why ChatGPT answers miss the point and how to fix them with follow-up prompts

Do not rewrite the whole prompt immediately. Identify the specific mismatch and ask for a focused revision.

August 3, 202610 min readBy Masaki Iino

This guide shows a practical way to use ChatGPT at work. The goal is not to memorize a magic prompt, but to give the AI enough context, constraints, and an output format that match the task.

You can copy the prompt example, replace the bracketed details, and then review the result before using it in real work.

Key takeaways

  • Compare the answer with the actual purpose before changing the prompt.
  • Name the exact problem: length, audience, tone, structure, or unsupported facts.
  • Keep useful content and change only what is wrong.
  • Ask the AI to separate facts, assumptions, and unknowns.
  • Restart only when the original task or source material was fundamentally wrong.

“Make it better” does not define what better means

Why a short instruction often fails

This is not good. Make it better.

A more reliable approach

Point to the mismatch and specify the change: shorten the background, write for a customer, put the conclusion first, or remove claims that are not supported by the source.

Information to provide

  • The original purpose of the answer
  • Who will read or use it
  • What is already useful and should remain
  • The exact section or behavior that is wrong
  • The desired length, tone, structure, and evidence standard

Reusable prompt template

Replace the bracketed parts with your situation. Ask the AI not to invent missing facts and to list questions separately.

Reusable prompt template
Review the answer below against the original task.

## Original task
[Paste the original request]

## Current answer
[Paste the answer]

## What needs to change
- Keep: [useful content]
- Change: [specific problem]
- Audience: [reader]
- Length: [target length]
- Structure: [desired order or headings]

## Accuracy rules
- Do not add facts that are not in the source material.
- Separate facts, suggestions, and points that require confirmation.
- Explain briefly which changes you made.

Return the revised answer only after applying these conditions.

Example: a customer reply is too apologetic and too long

The answer repeats the incident background, apologizes several times, and promises to update the customer “soon,” even though no update time is confirmed.

Practical example
Keep the confirmed facts and the commitment to contact the customer again. Reduce the answer to about half its length. Use one concise apology, remove repeated background, and replace “soon” with wording that does not promise an unconfirmed time. Put the next action in the final paragraph.

Practical example

Thank you for reporting the issue. We apologize for the disruption. Our technical team is currently investigating the cause. We cannot yet confirm a recovery time, but we will contact you again when verified information is available.

Review before using the result

Treat the result as a draft. Confirm names, dates, prices, commitments, and company policy before sending or acting on it.

  • The revision still answers the original task.
  • Useful facts were not removed with the unnecessary wording.
  • No new claim or promise was introduced.
  • The tone and vocabulary match the intended reader.
  • The final structure makes the next action easy to find.
Do not paste personal data, confidential customer information, contract details, or unreleased company information unless your organization allows it. Replace names and figures with placeholders when needed.

Frequently asked questions

Should I start a new chat when the answer is wrong?

Not always. A follow-up is effective when the source and task are correct and only the tone, length, structure, or focus needs adjustment.

How can I fix a response that mixes facts and guesses?

Ask for three sections: verified facts from the source, suggestions based on those facts, and information that cannot be confirmed.

What if several revisions make the answer worse?

Return to the original source, restate the purpose and non-negotiable conditions, and ask for an outline before another full draft.
