How to summarize long documents with ChatGPT for a specific business purpose

A useful summary is designed for a reader and a decision, not merely made shorter.

August 3, 202610 min readBy Masaki Iino

This guide shows a practical way to use ChatGPT at work. The goal is not to memorize a magic prompt, but to give the AI enough context, constraints, and an output format that match the task.

You can copy the prompt example, replace the bracketed details, and then review the result before using it in real work.

Key takeaways

  • Define who will read the summary and what they need to decide.
  • Specify the sections, maximum length, and information that must remain.
  • Ask the AI to distinguish the document’s claims from its own interpretation.
  • Use section or page references when traceability matters.
  • Check important figures, dates, exceptions, and recommendations against the source.

“Summarize this” does not identify what the reader needs to keep

Why a short instruction often fails

Summarize this document.

A more reliable approach

State the reader, purpose, target length, required sections, and the facts or exceptions that must not be omitted.

Information to provide

  • The full document or the relevant sections
  • The intended reader and their existing knowledge
  • The decision or action the summary should support
  • Required headings, length, and level of detail
  • A rule for citations, unknowns, and conflicting statements

Reusable prompt template

Replace the bracketed parts with your situation. Ask the AI not to invent missing facts and to list questions separately.

Reusable prompt template
## Task
Summarize the document below for [reader].

## Purpose
The reader needs the summary in order to [decision or action].

## Source
[Paste the text or attach the document]

## Output
1. Main conclusion
2. Key facts and figures
3. Business impact
4. Actions required
5. Risks, exceptions, and unresolved questions

## Constraints
- Keep the summary within [length].
- Do not add information that is not in the source.
- Distinguish the source’s statements from your interpretation.
- Preserve important dates, amounts, conditions, and exceptions.
- Include page or section references when available.

Example: a three-minute summary for a department head

A department head needs to review a long implementation proposal before deciding whether to approve a pilot. Background history is less important than cost, workload, risk, and the decision required.

Practical example
Summarize this proposal for a department head who has three minutes to review it. Use the sections: conclusion, expected benefit, cost and workload, risks, and decision required. Keep dates, amounts, dependencies, and conditions. Do not add recommendations that are not supported by the proposal.

Practical example

Conclusion: The proposal recommends a three-month pilot for one department.

Cost and workload: [verified amount] plus [verified staffing requirement].

Risks: Data migration and user adoption require mitigation plans.

Decision required: Approve or reject the pilot budget by [verified date].

Review before using the result

Treat the result as a draft. Confirm names, dates, prices, commitments, and company policy before sending or acting on it.

  • The summary reflects the requested reader and decision.
  • Important numbers, dates, conditions, and exceptions match the source.
  • Background was shortened without removing context needed for interpretation.
  • Opinions and recommendations are not presented as source facts.
  • A reviewer can trace critical statements back to the document.
Do not paste personal data, confidential customer information, contract details, or unreleased company information unless your organization allows it. Replace names and figures with placeholders when needed.

Frequently asked questions

Can ChatGPT summarize a PDF?

Depending on the AI service, you may be able to upload a PDF. Check whether tables, images, footnotes, and scanned pages were read correctly, especially when decisions depend on them.

How long should a summary be?

Choose the length based on use: a few bullets for a quick update, one page for a decision, or a structured section-by-section digest for detailed review.

How can I prevent important exceptions from disappearing?

Name the information that must remain, such as eligibility rules, exclusions, deadlines, dependencies, and conditions.
