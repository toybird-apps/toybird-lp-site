How to Use ChatGPT at Work: 5 Prompting Tips and 10 Practical Examples

What this guide will help you do

  • Give ChatGPT the source material instead of asking it to invent everything.
  • State the purpose, audience and constraints that matter.
  • Ask clarifying questions when information is missing.
  • Treat the first answer as a draft and improve only the parts that are off.

Apply it to a real task

Start with the source of truth you already use—an email thread, meeting record, worksheet, PDF, or system screen—rather than creating another layer of notes.

Ready-to-use prompt

Replace the bracketed text with your situation. Remove or anonymise confidential information, and verify the final output against the source before using it.

Ready-to-use prompt
Use only the information I provide below.
Task: [what you want done]
Purpose: [why the result is needed]
Audience: [who will read or use it]
Source material: [paste the email, notes or document]
Constraints: [tone, length, facts that must not be invented]
Output format: [email, table, bullets or another format]

If essential information is missing, ask up to three questions before drafting the answer.

Example: Turning a customer issue into a useful reply

A customer says data has not synced since this morning. The technical team is investigating, but there is no confirmed recovery time. The reply must apologise for the disruption without promising a deadline.

Subject: Update on the data sync issue

Thank you for contacting us. We are sorry for the disruption caused by the current sync issue. Our technical team is investigating the cause. We do not yet have a confirmed recovery time, but we will contact you again as soon as we have verified information.

Thank you for your patience.

Ways to adapt the prompt

The core structure stays the same, but the facts, audience and desired output should change with the task.

Create an email from bullet points

Provide the recipient, the facts to include, the required action and the deadline.

Turn meeting notes into actions

Ask for decisions, owners, due dates and unresolved items in separate sections.

Compare options

Define comparison criteria, priorities and how missing information should be handled.

Rewrite for another audience

Keep the facts but change vocabulary, detail and tone for the new reader.

Summarise a long document

Specify who will read the summary, what decision they need to make and how long it should be.

Prepare questions

Ask ChatGPT to find unclear assumptions and missing information in a proposal.

Research with sources

Request current, primary sources and separate verified facts from interpretation.

Extract tasks

Separate information to note from actions that someone must take.

Practise a sales conversation

Set the customer role, difficulty, one-question-at-a-time rule and feedback criteria.

Improve an existing answer

Name the exact problem: too long, wrong tone, unsupported fact or unclear conclusion.

Checklist before using the result

A polished answer can still contain an incorrect fact or an unsuitable commitment. Use this checklist before sharing or sending it.

  • Verify names, dates, prices and product details against the source.
  • Remove confidential or personal information unless your organisation allows it.
  • Make sure facts from the source are not mixed with AI suggestions.
  • Confirm that the next action is clear to the reader.
  • Keep the successful prompt and the final edits for reuse.

Frequently asked questions

Does a prompt have to be long?

No. It needs enough information for the task. A short prompt with clear purpose, source and constraints is often better than a long but unfocused one.

What should I do when the answer is wrong?

Describe the difference precisely. Ask for a shorter answer, a different tone, no unsupported facts or a specific output order.

Can ChatGPT ask me questions first?

Yes. Add a line asking it to request missing information before drafting. You can limit the number of questions or ask for one question at a time.
