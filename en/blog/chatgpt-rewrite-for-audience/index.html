How to rewrite text for customers, executives or internal teams with ChatGPT

Change the explanation, vocabulary, and emphasis while preserving the underlying facts.

August 3, 20269 min readBy Masaki Iino

This guide shows a practical way to use ChatGPT at work. The goal is not to memorize a magic prompt, but to give the AI enough context, constraints, and an output format that match the task.

You can copy the prompt example, replace the bracketed details, and then review the result before using it in real work.

Key takeaways

  • Define what the new reader already knows and what they need to do.
  • List the facts, terms, and caveats that must remain unchanged.
  • Specify tone, length, vocabulary, and desired call to action.
  • Ask the AI to identify information that cannot be adapted without confirmation.
  • Compare the rewrite with the source so meaning is not lost.

“Make this customer-friendly” can change the facts as well as the wording

Why a short instruction often fails

Rewrite this for a customer.

A more reliable approach

Describe the reader’s knowledge, purpose, tone, and next action, and state which facts, terms, numbers, and limitations must remain.

Information to provide

  • The original text and its verified facts
  • The new audience and what they already know
  • The purpose of the rewritten text
  • Tone, length, vocabulary, and structure
  • Details that must remain unchanged or require explanation

Reusable prompt template

Replace the bracketed parts with your situation. Ask the AI not to invent missing facts and to list questions separately.

Reusable prompt template
## Task
Rewrite the source text for [audience].

## Purpose
The reader should understand [main point] and then [next action].

## Source text
[Paste the text]

## Audience guidance
- Existing knowledge: [beginner / specialist / executive / customer]
- Tone: [formal / friendly / concise]
- Target length: [length]
- Terms to explain or avoid: [list]

## Preserve exactly
- [facts, numbers, product names, legal wording, limitations]

## Constraints
- Do not add benefits, claims, or promises that are not in the source.
- If the source is ambiguous, list the point for confirmation instead of rewriting it as a fact.
- Keep the meaning while changing the emphasis and wording.

Example: rewrite a technical feature note for a new customer

The source is written for internal engineers and focuses on implementation details. A new customer needs to understand the practical benefit, requirements, and limitation without technical jargon.

Practical example
Rewrite this release note for customers who are using the product for the first time. Explain the benefit first, use plain language, and keep the supported operating systems and known limitation exactly as written. Do not add performance claims. End with one clear next step.

Practical example

The update makes it easier to [verified user benefit]. It is available on [verified operating systems]. Before enabling it, note that [verified limitation]. To begin, open [verified location] and follow the setup steps.

Review before using the result

Treat the result as a draft. Confirm names, dates, prices, commitments, and company policy before sending or acting on it.

  • The rewrite preserves every important fact and limitation.
  • The new reader can understand the main point without internal jargon.
  • The tone is appropriate without becoming promotional or vague.
  • The call to action matches what the reader can actually do.
  • No unsupported benefit, comparison, or promise was added.
Do not paste personal data, confidential customer information, contract details, or unreleased company information unless your organization allows it. Replace names and figures with placeholders when needed.

Frequently asked questions

Is rewriting different from summarizing?

Yes. Rewriting changes how information is explained for a reader, while summarizing also reduces the amount of information. You can request both, but state which facts must remain.

How can I keep technical terms that are legally or operationally important?

List them under “Preserve exactly” and ask for a plain-language explanation beside them rather than replacing them.

Can I create several audience versions at once?

Yes. Ask for separate versions for each audience and define the purpose and maximum length of each version.

Adapt the explanation, not the verified facts

A strong audience rewrite changes vocabulary, order, and emphasis so the reader can act. It should not silently alter product limits, numbers, obligations, or other verified information.
