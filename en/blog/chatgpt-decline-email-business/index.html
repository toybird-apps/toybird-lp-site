How to write a polite refusal email with ChatGPT: client-ready prompt and examples

State what you cannot do, explain only what is necessary, and offer a realistic next step without creating a false promise.

August 3, 20269 min readBy Masaki Iino

This guide shows a practical way to use ChatGPT at work. The goal is not to memorize a magic prompt, but to give the AI enough context, constraints, and an output format that match the task.

You can copy the prompt example, replace the bracketed details, and then review the result before using it in real work.

Key takeaways

  • State the request you received and the exact part you cannot accept.
  • Give only the reason that is appropriate to share externally.
  • Offer an alternative only when it is genuinely available.
  • Keep the wording considerate, but do not make the decision ambiguous.
  • Check dates, commitments, and contract language before sending.

“Write a polite refusal” does not tell ChatGPT what can still be offered

Why a short instruction often fails

Write a polite email declining this request.

A more reliable approach

Provide the recipient, the original request, the non-negotiable limit, the reason you can share, and the alternative that is actually available.

Information to provide

  • The request from the client or partner
  • What cannot be accepted and what can still be done
  • A brief reason that may be shared externally
  • A realistic alternative date, scope, or method
  • The desired tone and the next question for the recipient

Reusable prompt template

Replace the bracketed parts with your situation. Ask the AI not to invent missing facts and to list questions separately.

Reusable prompt template
## Task
Write a reply that politely declines the business request below.

## Request received
[Paste the request]

## What we need to communicate
- We cannot: [state the specific limit]
- Reason we may share: [brief reason]
- Alternative we can offer: [date, scope, or method]
- What we need the recipient to confirm: [next step]

## Constraints
- Do not criticize the request or the person who made it.
- Make the limit clear rather than implying it may still be possible.
- Do not invent additional dates, discounts, or commitments.
- Use a calm, professional tone.

## Output
1. Subject line
2. Email body
3. Points to verify before sending

Example: declining an unexpected request for work this week

A client asks for additional work to be completed this week. The current team cannot do it this week, but can complete it next Wednesday.

Practical example
Write a formal reply to a client who asked us to complete unplanned additional work this week. We cannot complete it this week because of current capacity. We can complete it next Wednesday. Do not criticize the request. Clearly state the limit and propose next Wednesday as the alternative. Do not add any unconfirmed dates or promises.

Practical example

Subject: Timing for the additional work

Thank you for sending the details of the additional work. Based on our current schedule, we will not be able to complete it this week. We can, however, complete the work next Wednesday. Please let us know whether that timing works for you, and we will confirm the next steps.

Review before using the result

Treat the result as a draft. Confirm names, dates, prices, commitments, and company policy before sending or acting on it.

  • The refusal is clear and cannot be mistaken for a tentative answer.
  • The reason does not reveal unnecessary internal or customer information.
  • The alternative is available and has the correct date and scope.
  • The email does not accidentally accept liability or promise an outcome.
  • The recipient can see the next action they need to take.
Do not paste personal data, confidential customer information, contract details, or unreleased company information unless your organization allows it. Replace names and figures with placeholders when needed.

Frequently asked questions

Should I explain the refusal in detail?

Only explain what the recipient needs to understand the decision. Long internal explanations can create confusion or disclose information that should stay private.

How do I stop ChatGPT from inventing another alternative?

Add: “Do not add an alternative, date, discount, or commitment that is not written in the source information.”

Can a refusal sound warm without becoming vague?

Yes. Acknowledge the request and use considerate language, but state the limit directly, such as “We will not be able to complete this by Friday.”
